The project

The Commons is the name National Church Residences gave its supportive housing communities, starting with The Commons at Livingston in Columbus, Ohio. Each resident has a furnished home, a lease in their own name and support right in the building.

The challenge

For most residents it's the first home of their own in a long time, so the brand had to feel safe before anything else, and hopeful, with no labels: nobody here is defined by where they've been. Donors and partners read it too, so it also had to be structured and simple, never playful.

The idea

The chosen concept, Community, draws intersecting pathways for the services that bring each resident back into a safe community: healthcare, case management, peer support. Arches shaped like open doorways and small grids of dots build the rest, and the mark works for every new address.

My part

With the team at Zipcode Creative, I wrote the creative direction, directed the logo, and built the visual identity, the 36-page brand guide and a set of twelve icons. I selected the brand photography, too.

“…assurance that it's my home and my stuff won't disappear.”

The logo

One mark, many addresses

My part here was the logo direction: a simple, welcoming icon about community and belonging, with clean lines that never feel childish. I also asked for the location line to be set in a standard typeface, so the client can add each new property on their own. The final files already include seven locations.

The final brand

Doorways and dots

Six colors, from Cream to Espresso, structured and warm at the same time. Arches shaped like open doorways and small grids of dots build the patterns, and twelve icons point to what's waiting inside, from furnished homes to staff on site around the clock.
