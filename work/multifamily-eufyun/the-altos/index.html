The project

The Altos is a Class A building in White Plains, New York, with 407 residences, from studios to the only townhomes in its market, 35 minutes from Grand Central.

The challenge

White Plains was filling up with shiny new buildings. The Altos had something else: years of proof and a staff people talk about. The brand had to say that without raising its voice. Zipcode's copywriters put it in four words: proven, not just promised.

The idea

Altos means heights, so the identity looks up. Its rhythm comes from the building itself, the barcode pattern of the laser-cut metal privacy walls, turned into vertical bars, tall arches and a wordmark where the A stands taller than the other letters.

My part

With the team at Zipcode Creative, I wrote the creative direction, gave the direction for the logo, and designed the visual identity, the patterns and the 25-page brand guide. I selected the brand photography, too.

The logo

An A for heights

Altos means heights, and the whole Structural Rhythm direction grew from that: vertical bars, tall arches. I gave the direction for the logo, and in the final wordmark the A stands taller than the other letters and also works alone as a monogram.

The final brand

Arches and bars

Two patterns of arches and vertical bars, taken from the laser-cut metal privacy walls, each in six colors. The palette pairs a deep teal and copper with taupe, a pale blue, black and white.

The brand guide

25 pages, one standard

Archetypes, positioning, headlines, logos, colors, type, patterns and sample applications, for the property team. The words, including the tagline The Height Of White Plains Living, came from Zipcode's copywriters, and I designed every page.
