Floripa City Brand

City Branding · Branding · Visual Identity

A visual identity created for the city of Florianópolis as my final undergraduate project, which received the highest grade.

See it on Behance ↗

Developed as my final undergraduate project, which received the highest grade.

The project focuses on the city of Florianópolis, which didn't have a meaningful brand that showed its tangible and intangible qualities.

Project goal
Develop a flexible visual identity for the city of Florianópolis, promoting the region through a visual system that values its character and quirks.

Flexible visual identities?
Unlike the old corporate identity paradigm, which valued uniformity and consistency, the new brand paradigm trades those qualities for vibrancy and dynamism. “If people can change their clothes without changing their character... why can't brands?” Neumeier, 2013.

Gil (2011, p.102) argues that identity doesn't mean sameness: a dynamic logo doesn't ruin the brand, it creates new meanings and situations. The result is a rich visual language, with differences inside a cohesive, unified whole. According to Johnson (2013), flexible identities are ideal for brands that speak through many channels and languages, like Floripa, since they adapt to many situations and leave room for interpretation, understanding and questioning.

To sum up the brand's visual side, I put together a mood board, which, according to Lupton (2017), is essential to gather visual references and define the project's aesthetic.

The mood board brings together key visual elements such as colors, illustrations, patterns and typefaces, always in tune with the brand's positioning. The vibrant, cheerful, cozy colors bring to mind the sea, the sun, the sand and the architecture, strong parts of the local culture. The patterns have organic curves and soft, rounded shapes. The typefaces feel warm, close and friendly, to connect with the community.

The icon is minimal: it stands for the horizon, the sun, the sea, unity and community, plus a gear, a nod to technology.

The logo comes alive through its changing fill, first drawn as a pattern, with 42 variations, one for each officially listed beach in Floripa, while the icon and the typography stay fixed so the brand is always recognizable. Van Nes (2012) calls this kind of identity a container.
